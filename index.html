        Search help

        Medjat searches Crossref, a registry where publishers deposit descriptive records (metadata) for the articles, chapters and books they publish.

        What you can search for

        • A topic, title words, a place or a site. For example, Hierakonpolis excavation or Deir el-Medina ostraca.
        • One field. Use the menu beside the search box to search only titles, authors, editors, journal or book titles, publishers, institutions or funders. Institution and funder searches can only find records where the publisher listed them.
        • A DOI. Paste one, with or without the https://doi.org/ prefix, to open that exact record. Filters do not apply to DOI lookups.

        How matching works

        • Crossref finds records that contain any of your words and ranks the best matches first. It is not a phrase search, and quotation marks do not make it one. A few specific words usually work better than a long sentence.
        • Unless your search mentions Egypt or you choose a historical period, Medjat adds the words “ancient Egypt Egyptian” to your search. Crossref matches whole words, so Egyptian is included to catch works that never say Egypt. Turn this off with Add ancient Egypt terms under Search context in the filters.
        • Name fields also match any word, so a funder or institution search can return loosely related records. The added words above keep them on topic.

        Narrowing and sorting

        • Filter by publication year, publication type, open access, historical period, and which metadata a record has (abstract, ORCID, references).
        • Sort by relevance, newest, oldest, or most cited. Most cited only reorders the records already loaded.

        Getting better results

        • Try alternative spellings and transliterations.
        • Try the language of the scholarship you need.
        • Add a site, an object type or a person, or narrow the years, if a search is too broad.

        Once you find something

        Open a record to read its details and references, and save it to your Collection (stored in this browser). Then use Visualize to see patterns across the top results, or Places to find work that mentions a site.

        Medjat shows what Crossref holds. Crossref links DOIs to descriptive records very well, but it was not built as a subject bibliography. These are the limits to keep in mind.

        How Crossref returns results

        • Words, not subjects. Crossref has no label for “ancient Egypt” or for a period. Medjat finds work by matching words in descriptive fields, so it will miss some relevant work and include some that is not.
        • Totals run high. Crossref counts records matching any of your words, so a large total is the size of the candidate pool, not the amount of relevant scholarship.
        • Only part of a long list loads. Medjat asks for records at a time. Crossref never sends more than in one request, and date sorting stops after candidates. Narrow the search or the years to get past that.
        • Some controls work on loaded records only. Most cited and the open access filter reorder or narrow what has already loaded.
        • Charts are a sample. Visualize describes the first or ranked matches, not the whole literature.
        • A shared public service. Medjat paces its requests, so a search can occasionally be slow or fail; try again. Results are kept for five minutes, and Crossref’s records change as publishers update them.
        • Metadata only. Medjat searches records, not the text inside articles. A phrase that appears only in the body of a paper will not be found.

        What the metadata contains

        • Supplied by publishers. Publishers deposit the records and Crossref does not check or complete them, so detail varies by publisher.
        • Only works with a DOI. Many books, older articles, excavation reports and some regional publications have no DOI and do not appear.
        • Gaps are common. Abstracts, reference lists, ORCID iDs, licences, funders and author institutions exist for only some records. A missing field means nothing was deposited. It says nothing about quality or about whether other relevant work exists.
        • Records can be untidy. Authors may be missing, especially on book chapters. Titles may be in capitals. Translations, preprints and published versions can appear as separate records.
        • Dates are publication dates. They show when something was published, not the historical period it studies. Period filters are search words, not classifications.
        • Open access is a declaration. The badge means the publisher told Crossref the work carries an open licence. Medjat does not check it, and a DOI link can still lead to a page you cannot read.
        • Citations are partial. Cited-by counts only reflect reference lists that participating publishers deposited, and Crossref does not list the citing works. Retraction and correction notices appear only where they were deposited or recorded.
        • Places are suggestions. Crossref has no place data. Places looks for a site’s names in titles and abstracts, so a match is something to check, not proof that the work concerns that place.

        Looking further

        Treat results as a starting point. Check important claims against the publication itself, and search other catalogues for books and older scholarship. Results pages include a Search beyond Crossref list that runs your words in Google Scholar, WorldCat, Internet Archive Scholar and Open Library.
